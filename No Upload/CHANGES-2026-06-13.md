## Content to Add — ✅ information.html › Air & Health Section
*Source: PSD Air Construction Permit Application (80% Complete), April 23, 2026*

### 1. Specific Emission Numbers (HIGH PRIORITY)
The site mentions pollutants generally but lacks the actual figures. Add a table or list:
- Greenhouse gases (CO2e): **1,516,388 tons/year**
- Carbon monoxide (CO): **1,152 tons/year**
- Nitrogen oxides (NOx): **319 tons/year**
- Volatile organic compounds (VOC): **105 tons/year**
- Particulate matter (PM/PM10/PM2.5): **61.6 tons/year**
- Sulfur dioxide (SO2): **18 tons/year**
- Sulfuric acid mist (H2SO4): **2.7 tons/year**

### 2. Alliant's Own Modeling Exceeds Thresholds (HIGH PRIORITY)
*Source: PSD Class II Air Dispersion Modeling Protocol, April 20, 2026*
Alliant's own air dispersion modeling found that **PM10, PM2.5, NO2, and CO impacts will exceed monitoring de minimis levels** — meaning their own model predicts significant air quality impacts on the surrounding area.

### 3. Alliant Declined Independent Weather Monitoring (HIGH PRIORITY)
Residents asked to place temporary weather monitoring equipment near the proposed site. Alliant declined. Instead, Alliant is requesting to use the existing **Cedar Rapids Public Health monitor** — located in Cedar Rapids — as a substitute for on-site monitoring. Residents continue raising questions about whether site-specific monitoring will ultimately be required.

### 4. SCR vs. DLN — Cost Over Health Protection
*Source: PSD Air Construction Permit Application (80% Complete)*
Alliant evaluated Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) — a more effective NOx control technology — but chose cheaper Dry Low-NOx (DLN) burners instead. Alliant's own cost analysis calculated SCR at **~$89,389 per ton of NOx controlled** per turbine, and used cost as a factor in rejecting it. Carbon capture and sequestration was also evaluated and not proposed.
